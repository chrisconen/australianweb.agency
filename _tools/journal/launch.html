To start a free online store, you need more than an attractive front page. Customers must understand the product, choose the right option and complete an order that your business can actually fulfil. These seven steps turn that goal into a manageable launch plan.

YOUR STARTING POINT

The Neocart base shop software costs A$0. AWA’s agreed setup is A$999 once, plus GST, with hosting and domain separate. The launch date depends on scope, supplied content and the readiness of your payment and delivery providers.

1. Choose a manageable launch catalogue

Begin with the products you can describe, photograph and supply reliably. A smaller complete catalogue is usually easier to review than a large collection of partial entries. Decide who will answer product questions and maintain prices and stock after launch.

For each product, collect its name, reference or SKU, price, description, images, available quantity and variants. Add the information that matters for your particular range, such as size, material or delivery restrictions. Use consistent names so the person packing an order can recognise the exact item.

The AWA setup includes initial entry of the first 20 products from your supplied information. Choose representative products, including a typical variant, so the review covers more than a single easy example.

2. Give the store a clear visual direction

Bring your logo, preferred colours and examples of the mood you want to create. The goal is a coherent store that feels like your business. A visitor should quickly understand what you sell and what makes the products worth considering.

Review a complete product page with real information early. Long names, several variants and different image shapes reveal practical layout needs that a perfect placeholder can hide. Check the mobile view as carefully as the desktop presentation.

You can plan directly with AWA in English. The optional external Neocart planner currently has a Hungarian interface. It is a planning option, not a required step or a live shop on your domain. Your storefront language and content are agreed in the written scope.

3. Confirm domain, hosting and ownership

Decide whether the shop will use an existing domain or a new one. Identify who controls the registrar account and who can approve changes. Domain connection is part of the agreed setup; registration and renewal are separate costs.

The hosting offer should explain the service being provided and the contact for problems. Discuss backups, restoration and updates before launch. A list of systems, owners and renewal dates is a useful starting point for operating the store.

Arrange a secure way to provide any necessary access. Passwords and private provider keys do not belong in a public product spreadsheet or a shared design mock-up.

4. Map payment, delivery and order handling

Choose the methods you genuinely need at launch. Where will you deliver? Will customers collect orders? What should happen when payment is pending rather than complete? Identify the person responsible for each stage.

Merchant and delivery accounts remain yours. AWA sets up the connections confirmed in your scope; provider availability, account approval and fees are checked before the build. A free software licence is not a substitute for those contracts.

Include the invoicing and bookkeeping workflow in this discussion. A successful-looking checkout is not enough if the team cannot distinguish paid, unpaid and cancelled orders. Each state should have a clear next action.

5. Review the product and customer information

Check that product descriptions are accurate and that you have the right to use the supplied images and text. Make the practical details easy to find: what is included, which variant is selected and how delivery works.

Have the policies, customer information and relevant legal or tax settings reviewed for your actual business and selling regions. Give your adviser the proposed checkout flow as well as the written policies, so the review covers what customers really see.

A general launch guide cannot establish compliance for an individual business. The useful outcome here is a clear owner for each required document and setting, with unresolved questions identified before publication.

6. Place a complete test order

Walk through a realistic purchase on a phone and a computer. Check more than whether the buttons respond. The selected product, variant, quantity, delivery option and amount must remain consistent all the way into the order record.

7. Approve, hand over and watch the first days

After the review and your approval, the store can go live on the agreed domain. Use the handover to practise everyday tasks with actual examples: adding a product, changing a price, checking stock and handling an order.

In the first days, review open orders and common customer questions. If buyers repeatedly ask the same thing, improve the relevant product page or delivery explanation. Keep a small list of practical improvements and work through them in order.

Send more traffic to the store when the team is comfortable with the order process. Marketing budget and acquisition strategy are separate decisions; a free online store does not guarantee demand or a particular sales result.

What to include in your first brief

  • What your business sells and who buys it.
  • Approximate product count and common variants.
  • Your existing domain or current store, if any.
  • Available logo, photography and product descriptions.
  • Preferred payment, delivery and accounting workflow.
  • Who will manage products and orders after launch.
